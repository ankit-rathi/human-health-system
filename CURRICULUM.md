# Curriculum: The Human Health System

## Learning objective

Move learners from fragmented health information to a connected,
practical understanding of human health.

The curriculum is organised into six parts. The order matters:
understand health and the body first, then the foundations, their
interactions, personal health management and the changing health
landscape.

## Part I --- Understanding Health

**Central question:** What do we mean by health, and how can we
understand it?

Topics: - Health, wellness, disease, risk and resilience - Health as a
dynamic system rather than a fixed state - Prevention, screening and
healthcare - Population evidence versus individual experience - Health
measurements: what they represent and what they do not - Risk,
uncertainty, correlation and causation - Health literacy and
misinformation

**Learner outcome:** Explain health as a multidimensional, changing
state and distinguish a measurement from an interpretation or diagnosis.

## Part II --- Understanding the Human Body

**Central question:** How does the body maintain itself and respond to
change?

Topics: - Cells, tissues, organs and organ systems - Homeostasis and
feedback loops - Energy, metabolism and adaptation - Digestive system
and nutrient absorption - Cardiovascular and respiratory systems -
Musculoskeletal system - Nervous and endocrine systems - Immune function
and inflammation - Genetics, ageing and individual variation

**Learner outcome:** Describe the body's major systems and understand
why a change in one area can affect another.

## Part III --- The Foundations of Health

**Central question:** Which everyday inputs and behaviours support human
function?

### 1. Nutrition

-   Energy balance and body composition
-   Macronutrients and micronutrients
-   Food quality, dietary patterns and fibre
-   Digestion, gut function and hydration
-   Meal patterns and individual needs
-   Supplements: indications, limitations and safety

### 2. Movement

-   Physical activity and sedentary behaviour
-   Cardiovascular fitness
-   Strength and muscular function
-   Mobility, balance and functional capacity
-   Exercise adaptation, progression and recovery
-   Safe participation and professional support

### 3. Sleep and recovery

-   Sleep architecture and circadian rhythm
-   Sleep quantity and quality
-   Recovery and adaptation
-   Fatigue and common sleep disruptors

### 4. Mind and stress

-   Stress physiology
-   Emotional regulation
-   Mental well-being
-   Relaxation, breathing and mindfulness practices
-   When professional mental-health support is needed

### 5. Relationships and social context

-   Social connection and health
-   Family, work and community influences
-   Behavioural support and accountability

### 6. Environment

-   Food and built environments
-   Air quality, light and noise
-   Sunlight and outdoor activity
-   Work patterns and digital environments
-   Access, culture and socioeconomic context

**Learner outcome:** Explain the major foundations and recognise that
their relevance and implementation differ between people.

## Part IV --- How the Foundations Interact

**Central question:** Why can health not be improved by treating each
lifestyle factor in isolation?

Topics: - Energy balance, appetite and body composition - Metabolic and
cardiovascular health - Blood pressure and vascular function - Muscle,
bone and functional ageing - Digestion and gut health - Sleep, stress,
appetite and activity - Physical activity, glucose regulation and
fitness - Inflammation: normal function, measurement and limitations -
Lifestyle patterns, cumulative effects and individual variation

**Learner outcome:** Build connected explanations rather than assuming
one food, supplement, exercise or metric determines health.

## Part V --- Managing Your Health

**Central question:** How can people turn understanding into sustainable
decisions?

Topics: - Goal-setting and behaviour change - Habit design and
environmental cues - Building realistic routines - Understanding common
health measurements - Trends, baselines, variability and measurement
error - Preventive care and screening discussions - Preparing for
clinical appointments - Shared decision-making and professional
referrals - Adapting routines to life circumstances - Setbacks,
maintenance and long-term consistency

**Learner outcome:** Design and review general health habits while
recognising when clinical guidance is required.

## Part VI --- Health in a Changing World

**Central question:** How do we remain informed and make good decisions
as health and technology evolve?

Topics: - Healthy ageing and maintaining function - Wearables and
consumer health technology - Personal health data and privacy -
Artificial intelligence in health information - Misinformation,
marketing and conflicts of interest - New research and changing
guidelines - Personalisation: promise and limitations - Lifelong
learning and health literacy

**Learner outcome:** Evaluate new claims and technologies thoughtfully
without confusing novelty with evidence.

## Curriculum design rules

-   Teach prerequisites before advanced concepts.
-   Explain normal function before discussing dysfunction.
-   Introduce disease concepts for health literacy, not to teach
    self-diagnosis.
-   Connect every foundation to at least one other foundation.
-   Include everyday examples and practical context.
-   Give greater depth to concepts with greater explanatory value.
-   Revisit key concepts as the learner progresses.
-   Keep the curriculum evidence-informed and periodically reviewed.

## Chapter continuity contract

Every chapter should answer:

1.  What fundamental question does this chapter answer?
2.  What should the learner be able to explain afterwards?
3.  Why does this knowledge matter in everyday life?
4.  Which earlier concept does it depend on?
5.  What question naturally leads to the next chapter?

A chapter is not complete merely because it contains information. It is
complete when it advances the learner's mental model.
