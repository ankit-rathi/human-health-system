# Roadmap and Execution Plan

## Strategic direction

Build an independent, education-first health platform alongside current employment.

The first priority is intellectual credibility and useful teaching. Audience growth, products, community and business infrastructure should follow demonstrated educational value.

The organisation should borrow useful delivery ideas from established health-education programmes—structured journeys, accountability, multidisciplinary expertise and thoughtful measurement—without prematurely copying a clinical transformation model.

## Stage 0 — Establish the foundation

**Objective:** Agree what the platform stands for and how it will operate.

Deliverables:

- Vision and positioning.
- Six-part curriculum.
- Capsule format and research workflow.
- Evidence and professional-scope policy.
- Initial capsule inventory and dependency map.
- A clear distinction between education, coaching, clinical care and technology.

**Exit test:** Can a new topic be placed in the curriculum, researched, explained and reviewed consistently?

## First 90 days — Build and test the learning method

### Weeks 1–2: Align

- Review and finalise the six repository documents.
- Define the first 30 capsule titles.
- Identify prerequisite relationships and cross-connections.
- Establish a simple source log and review process.

### Weeks 3–4: Prioritise

- Select 4–6 pilot capsules that teach foundational concepts.
- Prefer concepts that unlock several later topics.
- Include at least one body-process concept and one lifestyle-interaction concept.
- Define the learner question and intended outcome for each pilot.

### Weeks 5–8: Create

- Research and draft the pilot capsules.
- Create original visual explanations.
- Test whether the explanation works in short written and spoken formats.
- Seek qualified review for clinical or safety-sensitive claims.
- Publish a small number of finished pieces.

### Weeks 9–12: Test

- Run a small free educational session or learning experiment.
- Ask learners to explain concepts in their own words.
- Identify where visuals, analogies or terminology caused confusion.
- Test whether learners can connect one concept to another.
- Refine the capsule template and production workflow.

**90-day success:** A validated teaching method, a small set of high-quality capsules and evidence that learners understand more—not a follower target or company launch.

## Year 1 — Build intellectual credibility

Objectives:

- Study foundational health science systematically.
- Develop approximately 30–40 carefully researched capsules.
- Publish consistently in visual, written and video formats.
- Build a traceable source library.
- Develop relationships with qualified reviewers.
- Learn which explanations genuinely improve understanding.
- Identify areas where additional training or formal study is necessary.

Potential outputs:

- A coherent starter knowledge library.
- A recognisable educational voice.
- A repeatable capsule-to-content workflow.
- A clear list of curriculum gaps and expert-review needs.

## Year 2 — Build the learning experience

Objectives:

- Develop a book manuscript from the strongest connected capsules.
- Organise the material into a guided introductory course.
- Offer free learning sessions and small workshops.
- Build an email list and a modest learning community.
- Introduce structured exercises, reflection and comprehension checks.
- Collect feedback on learning outcomes, not just satisfaction.

Potential outputs:

- Book draft.
- Introductory course.
- Workshop format.
- A repeatable learning experience.
- Evidence of repeat interest and learner progress.

## Year 3 — Establish the education platform

Objectives:

- Publish the book or a substantial first guide.
- Introduce paid self-paced courses and workshops where demand exists.
- Develop a community or membership model only if it improves learning.
- Formalise clinical review and referral partnerships.
- Explore corporate health-literacy education.
- Evaluate whether a small team is justified.
- Consider simple educational tools only after the learning model is proven.

Potential outputs:

- Multiple educational products.
- Repeat learners and customers.
- Documented learning outcomes.
- A sustainable operating model with appropriate governance.

## Longer term — Build an organisation

Potential directions:

- Expand the curriculum and course catalogue.
- Invite qualified professionals to teach specialist topics.
- Develop educator or facilitator partnerships with clear safeguards.
- Create responsible educational technology for learning and health-data literacy.
- Build a community around lifelong health literacy.
- Expand only where quality, governance and economics can be maintained.

## Programme-design principles worth adopting

A useful learning programme should have:

1. **A clear journey:** learners know where they are, what they are learning and what comes next.
2. **Small achievable steps:** concepts and activities are sequenced rather than delivered as an overwhelming information dump.
3. **Reinforcement:** learners revisit ideas, practise explaining them and connect them to daily life.
4. **Feedback:** questions, reflection and comprehension checks reveal where understanding is weak.
5. **Community:** peer discussion can support motivation, provided it does not become a source of unqualified medical advice.
6. **Expert involvement:** qualified professionals review or teach within their competence.
7. **Outcome measurement:** assess learning, confidence and decision quality without claiming that educational participation caused clinical improvement.

These are educational design principles. They do not authorise the platform to provide clinical treatment or medication management.

## Weekly operating model

An initial target of approximately 5–7 hours per week:

| Activity | Weekly time |
|---|---:|
| Study and source review | 2 hours |
| Capsule research and writing | 1.5 hours |
| Visual creation | 1 hour |
| Video and publishing | 1 hour |
| Feedback and planning | 0.5 hour |

Adjust the workload to protect family time, current employment and personal well-being. Consistency matters more than an ambitious schedule that cannot be sustained.

## Measures of progress

### Knowledge quality

- Capsules with traceable sources.
- Accuracy and review status.
- Important claims appropriately qualified.
- Corrections made transparently.

### Learning impact

- Learner comprehension before and after.
- Ability to explain concepts in their own words.
- Ability to connect concepts across the curriculum.
- Return participation and course completion.
- Feedback on clarity and usefulness.

### Audience and community

- Returning learners.
- Newsletter engagement.
- Workshop participation.
- Meaningful questions and referrals.

### Business sustainability

- Paid conversion and repeat purchases.
- Revenue diversity.
- Cost and time per educational product.
- Dependence on the founder's personal time.
- Ability to maintain quality as the audience grows.

Do not optimise for vanity metrics at the expense of learning quality.

## Financial and career guardrails

- Retain current employment while validating the platform.
- Avoid major upfront spending on branding, offices, apps or employees.
- Use small experiments to test demand.
- Check employer policies on outside work, conflicts of interest, intellectual property and use of company resources.
- Keep project work, accounts, devices and intellectual property separate from the employer.
- Do not leave employment based on views, followers or one successful launch.
- Reassess only after repeatable demand, sustainable economics and adequate personal financial resilience are demonstrated.

## Decision gates

Before moving to the next stage, ask:

1. Is the educational content trustworthy and useful?
2. Can learners demonstrate improved understanding?
3. Is there evidence of repeat interest?
4. Can the next product be delivered without compromising quality?
5. Are professional boundaries and governance adequate?
6. Is the business model sustainable without relying entirely on the founder's time?

Grow when the evidence supports growth—not simply because the calendar says it is time.
