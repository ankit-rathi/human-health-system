# Roadmap and Execution Plan

## Strategic direction

Build an independent, education-first health platform while retaining
the founder's current employment.

The first priority is intellectual credibility and useful teaching.
Audience growth, products and business infrastructure should follow
demonstrated educational value.

## Stage 0 --- Set the foundation

**Objective:** Define what the platform stands for and how it will work.

Deliverables: - Vision and positioning. - Six-part curriculum. - Capsule
template and editorial workflow. - Evidence and professional-scope
policy. - Initial topic and dependency map.

Exit test: Can the project be explained clearly, and can a new capsule
be created consistently using the agreed method?

## First 90 days --- Pilot the learning method

### Weeks 1--2: Define

-   Finalise the manifesto and positioning.
-   Define the six foundations and how they interact.
-   Establish source, evidence and scope standards.

### Weeks 3--4: Map

-   Draft an initial list of 30 capsule titles.
-   Map prerequisites and cross-connections.
-   Select 10 foundational pilot capsules.

### Weeks 5--8: Create

-   Research and draft 4--6 capsules.
-   Create original visual explanations.
-   Seek qualified review for clinical or safety-sensitive claims.
-   Publish the first few pieces.

### Weeks 9--12: Test

-   Continue a sustainable publishing rhythm.
-   Run a small free educational session.
-   Ask learners what they understood and what remains confusing.
-   Test comprehension, not only satisfaction.
-   Refine the capsule format and workflow.

90-day success is a validated learning method and a small number of
high-quality published pieces---not a follower target or company launch.

## Year 1 --- Build intellectual credibility

Objectives: - Study foundational health science systematically. -
Develop the first 40--50 capsules. - Publish consistently in visual,
written and video formats. - Build a source library and professional
review relationships. - Learn how everyday learners respond to different
explanations.

Potential outputs: - A public knowledge library. - A consistent
educational identity. - A small set of well-tested teaching formats. - A
clearer view of where further formal education or practical training is
useful.

## Year 2 --- Build the learning experience

Objectives: - Develop the book manuscript. - Organise capsules into a
coherent course. - Launch a free introductory learning journey. - Run
educational workshops. - Build an email list and small learning
community. - Measure comprehension and learner feedback.

Potential outputs: - Book draft. - Introductory course. - Workshop
format. - Repeatable content production process.

## Year 3 --- Establish the education platform

Objectives: - Publish the book or a first substantial guide. - Introduce
paid self-paced courses and workshops. - Develop a community or
membership model if there is demonstrated demand. - Formalise clinical
review and referral partnerships. - Explore corporate health-literacy
education. - Evaluate whether a small team is justified.

Potential outputs: - Multiple educational products. - Recurring learners
and customers. - Documented learning outcomes. - A sustainable operating
model.

## Longer term --- Build an organisation

Potential directions: - Expand the curriculum and course catalogue. -
Invite qualified experts to teach specialist topics. - Develop
facilitator or educator partnerships with appropriate safeguards. -
Create responsible educational technology. - Build a community around
lifelong health literacy. - Expand only where educational quality and
governance can be maintained.

## Weekly operating model

An initial target of approximately 5--7 hours per week:

  Activity                         Weekly time
  ------------------------------ -------------
  Study and source review              2 hours
  Capsule research and writing       1.5 hours
  Visual creation                       1 hour
  Video and publishing                  1 hour
  Feedback and planning               0.5 hour

Adjust the workload to protect family time, current employment and
personal health.

## Measures of progress

### Knowledge quality

-   Capsules with traceable sources.
-   Accuracy and review status.
-   Concepts explained clearly.
-   Corrections made transparently.

### Learning impact

-   Learner comprehension before and after.
-   Ability to explain concepts in their own words.
-   Course completion and return participation.
-   Feedback on clarity and usefulness.

### Audience and community

-   Returning learners.
-   Newsletter engagement.
-   Workshop participation.
-   Meaningful questions and referrals.

### Business sustainability

-   Paid conversion and repeat purchases.
-   Revenue diversity.
-   Cost and time per educational product.
-   Dependence on the founder's personal time.

Do not optimise for vanity metrics at the expense of learning quality.

## Financial and career guardrails

-   Retain current employment while validating the platform.
-   Avoid major upfront spending on branding, offices, apps or
    employees.
-   Use small experiments to test demand.
-   Check employer policies on outside work, conflicts of interest,
    intellectual property and use of company resources.
-   Keep project work, accounts, devices and intellectual property
    separate from the employer.
-   Do not leave employment based on views, followers or one successful
    launch.
-   Reassess only after repeatable demand, sustainable economics and
    adequate personal financial resilience are demonstrated.

## Decision gates

Before moving to the next stage, ask:

1.  Is the educational content trustworthy and useful?
2.  Can learners demonstrate improved understanding?
3.  Is there evidence of repeat interest?
4.  Can the next product be delivered without compromising quality?
5.  Are professional boundaries and governance adequate?
6.  Is the business model sustainable without relying entirely on the
    founder's time?

Grow when the evidence supports growth---not simply because the calendar
says it is time.
